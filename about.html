Andamana is a working boat operator, not a booking platform. Everything on this site leaves from a pier we stand on, with a captain we know by name.

How this started

We were the boat
the hotels called
when the fleet
was full.

Two long-tails pulled up on Tonsai beach, a hand-painted sign and a rule we still run on: leave before everyone else. In 2009 that meant 06:30 starts and a lot of very confused guests. It also meant Pileh Lagoon to ourselves, which is the only reason anybody booked us twice.

Sixteen seasons later there are thirty-four boats on the roster across twelve piers, and the rule has not changed. Every skipper grew up on the water they sail. Nobody on our staff reads from a script, because nobody wrote one.

Safety at sea

The boring part
that matters

The Andaman is a benign sea for about seven months a year and a serious one for the rest. Here is exactly how we handle that.

01

Every seat, a jacket

Coastguard-rated buoyancy aids sized for adults and children, plus two throw lines and a first-aid kit on every hull. Checked at the pier, not in an office.

02

The 18:00 call

We read the forecast at six the evening before and either confirm or move you. Weather cancellations are refunded in full — we would rather lose the day than the boat.

03

Licensed, insured, small

Thai Marine Department licences on every vessel, passenger insurance on every ticket, and a hard cap of what one boat carries comfortably rather than legally.

Marine park fees

Money that
is not ours

Most of the places worth going are inside a national park, and the park charges admission at the buoy line: 400 baht for Phi Phi Leh, 300 for Hong and Phang Nga, 200 for Tarutao. Children pay half. It is collected in cash, by a ranger, from a boat.

We list it separately on every trip instead of folding it into the headline price. It funds the mooring buoys that keep anchors off the coral, so it is worth paying without complaint — but you should know what you are handing over and why.

  • Phi Phi Leh — 400 B adult / 200 B child
  • Hong Islands & Phang Nga — 300 B adult
  • Tarutao & Koh Lipe — 200 B adult
  • Similan liveaboard — included in the fare

Season & weather

When to come,
honestly

Nov – Feb

High season, flat water

Northeast monsoon, which the Andaman coast sits in the lee of. Glass mornings, 30°C, and every boat booked a week out. Come now if you have never done this before.

Mar – Apr

Hot, clear, quieter

The best underwater visibility of the year and the best chance of a manta at Koh Bon. Also 36°C on the sand — bring more water than you think.

May – Jun

Shoulder, half price

Squalls that pass in an hour and long clear spells between them. Private boats drop by up to forty per cent. Our own favourite time to be out.

Jul – Oct

Southwest monsoon

Real swell on the west coast, and some routes close entirely. We keep running the sheltered east-coast and mangrove trips, and we will tell you plainly when the answer is no.
